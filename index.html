        The UX principles behind every screen

        Start from the user's goal

        The front door shows login plus the 5 most common tasks. The dashboard opens with your next action, not a menu.

        Task-first information architecture

        Fewer choices, faster decisions

        5 login types become 1 (others behind a link). 18 tax acronyms become one plain question.

        Hick's law

        Recognise, don't remember

        The period code is generated, the tax type is filled in, and your date of birth is read from your NIC. No codes to look up.

        Recognition over recall (Nielsen #6)

        Prevent errors before they happen

        Checks as you type, a warning before account lockout, and rules like the 7-day PIN limit shown before you submit.

        Error prevention (Nielsen #5)

        Always show where you are

        Progress steps on every form, a live return tracker, and an instant receipt.

        Visibility of system status (Nielsen #1)

        Speak the user's language

        “My own income tax”, not “IIT”. Every remaining term has a tooltip. One name, TIN, everywhere.

        Match with the real world · Consistency (Nielsen #2, #4)

        Show details only when needed

        Business login, the tax working-out and Sinhala/Tamil name fields stay hidden until you need them.

        Progressive disclosure

        Works for everyone

        Sinhala, Tamil and English. No image captcha, any browser, 44px touch targets, keyboard and screen-reader friendly, AA colour contrast, reduced motion respected.

        Built toward WCAG 2.2 AA

        Make people feel in control

        “You're on track”, a progress ring for the year, and a real “done” moment after paying. Tax shouldn't feel scary.

        Do you have a TIN?

        Your TIN is your 9-digit tax number. You don't need a PIN or a login to pay at a bank.

        You need a TIN before you can pay. Get a TIN. It's free and takes about 10 minutes, and you can come straight back here.

        What are you paying?
        You probably don't need to pay anything yourself. If your only income is your salary and your employer deducts APIT every month, that is usually your final tax. You may still need to file a return if you have other income. Check with the estimator.

          e-Services › File my tax return

          File my tax return

          Do I need to file?

          • Yes, if you have business, freelance, rental or investment income.
          • Yes, if you paid quarterly instalments.
          • Usually no, if your only income is a salary with APIT deducted and you claim no extra reliefs.

          What you need

          • Your TIN and PIN
          • Your APIT certificate from your employer
          • Records of other income and any instalments you paid

          How to file

          1. Log in to e-Services.
          2. Choose File return → Income Tax → Year of assessment.
          3. Fill in your income and reliefs. The tax is calculated for you.
          4. Submit, then pay any balance using Pay income tax.
